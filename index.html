What makes a prior moral case relevant?

An exploratory case-relevance exercise built around one workplace accountability dilemma and eight candidate prior cases from DailyDilemmas. This is a research walkthrough, not a formal participant study.

01 / Research question

Why might one case help us reason about another?

We are exploring whether perceived relevance depends on shared moral values, correspondence in the underlying relations between actors, or similarity in institutional setting. These are proposed distinctions to validate, not already-proven independent factors.

A / VALUE

Which moral concern?

Accountability is present in the target; safety is our contrasting value. A label match alone does not guarantee that cases share a precise normative principle.

B / DEPTH

How are the roles and relations aligned?

Deep: an observer encounters another person's concerning conduct and considers escalating it to an authority. Shallow: the decision follows a different relational pattern.

C / DISTANCE

How similar is the setting?

Near: a workplace or professional organizational setting. Far: an educational, friendship, family, or social context outside ordinary workplace roles.

02 / Fixed reference point

Target: workplace accountability

AccountabilityWorkplaceDailyDilemmas · #55

You're a project manager in a marketing firm. You notice a colleague using company resources for a personal project during office hours, undermining the company's policy. Should you take initiative and report this to your superior?

Why accountability? It operates at two levels: the colleague's answerability for their use of shared resources, and the project manager's potential responsibility to address or report the conduct.
Proposed relational structureAn observer becomes aware of another person's concerning conduct and must decide whether to escalate it to an authority, potentially imposing consequences on that person.

05 / Interpretation

What this prototype can, and cannot, establish

Working hypotheses, not validated manipulations

Safety and accountability can co-occur; the contrast is not necessarily exclusive. “Deep” cases differ in how the observer learns about the conduct, and “near” professional contexts (such as restaurants and software teams) are not identical to marketing firms. Severity, uncertainty, and personal relationships also vary. A separate pilot should test perceived value similarity, relational alignment, contextual distance, and severity before treating these as orthogonal factors. A relevance rating alone cannot identify which feature caused the rating.
